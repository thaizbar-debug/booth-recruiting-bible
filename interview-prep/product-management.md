## Product Questions

**What is your favorite product of ours?**
→ Research deeply. Know the product intimately — have used it. Have 3 likes, 3 dislikes, and a specific improvement ready.

**What would you improve about [product]?**
→ Framework: (1) Who is the target user? (2) What job are they hiring this product to do? (3) Where is there friction? (4) What's your hypothesis for improvement? (5) How would you test it?

**Tell me about a product you like. Size the US market for it.**
→ Market sizing framework: Top-down (TAM → SAM → SOM) or bottom-up (users × usage × value per use). Practice this for 2-3 products before interviews. Be comfortable with the math.

**How will you redesign [X] product?**
→ Start with user needs, not features. "Before I redesign anything, I'd want to understand the current user's biggest frustration. If I had to guess, I'd say it's [specific hypothesis]. Here's how I'd test that before building..."

## Mini-Cases (Practice for Amazon, Google, tech PM roles)

**Why does Amazon let 3rd party sellers use its platform?**
→ Network effects, selection breadth, fee revenue, competitive moat against niche retailers.

**How would you expand Amazon to a new country?**
→ Framework: Market attractiveness (size, growth, logistics infrastructure, payment systems), competitive dynamics, regulatory environment, fulfillment economics, go-to-market approach. LATAM: strong case for Colombia, Chile, or Peru (growing middle class, smartphone penetration, underserved logistics).

**If you were PM on Kindle and a technical bug would delay launch for Christmas?**
→ Triage: How severe? What's the UX impact? Can we ship with a reduced feature set? What's the revenue impact of a 2-week delay vs. shipping broken? Communicate transparently to leadership.

**Sales are falling for [product] — what would you do?**
→ Diagnose first: Is it acquisition, activation, retention, or revenue? Segment by user type. Check competitive moves. Only then recommend action.

**Explain what would happen if we raised the price of Amazon Prime by $10.**
→ Price elasticity analysis: What % of users are price-sensitive? What is Prime's substitution cost (alternative services)? Likely result: marginal churn from price-sensitive users, higher revenue from retained users. Long-term: depends on whether the service value keeps pace with price.

## Technical Questions (for Technical PM roles)

**What is HTML5 / SQL / [tech concept]?**
→ Refresh basics. Engineering background = you can handle this. Be precise.

**Walk me through how Google search works.**
→ Crawling → indexing → ranking (PageRank + 200+ signals) → query → SERP. Add: personalization signals, local results, knowledge graph.

**How would you prioritize a feature you want to add?**
→ Framework: Impact (user value + business value) vs. Effort (engineering cost) + Strategic alignment. RICE or ICE scoring. Always tie back to the user need the feature serves.

**Trade-off: Two projects, same deadline, limited resources — which do you finish?**
→ Decision framework: Strategic value, contractual obligations, user impact, team morale, signal sent to market. "I'd first check if there's a way to scope down one project rather than abandon either. If not: [specific criteria I'd use to decide]."

## Out-of-the-Box Questions

**How long would it take to fly everyone on Earth to the moon?**
→ Fermi estimation. State assumptions clearly, work through the math, arrive at an order-of-magnitude answer. Process matters more than precision.

**If you were the Google CEO, what 3 problems would keep you awake at night?**
→ (1) AI search disruption (ChatGPT eating search share). (2) Regulatory antitrust pressure globally. (3) YouTube content quality and advertiser brand safety. Show you've thought about Google's actual challenges.
