# Interview Prep — General Questions (All Tracks)

Source: Booth Career Services FAQ (General-Alumni)
These questions appear in virtually every interview regardless of track. Prepare them once, adapt for every room.

---

## The Questions You Will Always Get

### Open-Ended

**Tell me about yourself. Walk me through your resume.**
→ This is your most important answer. Practice until it flows naturally in under 2 minutes.

**Structure:**
1. Who you are + where you're from (brief — Peru, engineering + business degrees)
2. First job and what you learned (Favo — youngest PM, startup chaos, B2B product)
3. The deliberate move (BCP — financial inclusion, base-of-the-pyramid, Warda launch)
4. The even more deliberate move (50% pay cut, consulting at Apoyo — chose discomfort for skill-building)
5. Why Booth, and why now (Strategic Management + Marketing, Chicago ecosystem, MBA to operate at higher scale)
6. What you're targeting and why (consulting and corporate strategy — applying rigorous problem-solving to real business challenges)

**Draft opening:** "I'm Thaiz Barthelmess. I grew up in Peru, studied both engineering and business, and started my career as a PM — I was the youngest at Favo, Peru's second highest-funded startup, at 22. After navigating a major company restructuring there, I moved to BCP, Peru's largest bank, where I built Warda — a gamified savings app for low-income communities. Then I made a deliberate choice to leave a well-paying PM role to take a 50% pay cut and join Apoyo, Peru's most rigorous consulting firm — because I wanted to develop structured problem-solving from the inside. I'm at Booth now, concentrating in Strategic Management and Marketing, targeting consulting and corporate strategy roles where I can work on the hardest problems at companies that matter."

---

**Tell me about an experience where you had to make a difficult decision.**
→ Consulting pivot (50% pay cut) or Favo triage decision (which products to save). Both show deliberate decision-making under constraint.

**Tell me about your last experience working in a team.**
→ BCP — 30-person cross-functional team. Designed the auction system to create alignment. Or Apoyo consulting team.

**Why consulting? Marketing? [Function]?**
→ See track-specific files for tailored answers.

**Convince me I should hire you.**
→ "Three things that I think are genuinely rare in an MBA cohort: First, I have actual PM experience — I shipped products to real users at scale, not just participated in strategy projects. Second, I have consulting experience, which means I know how to structure a problem, not just execute within one. Third, I have deep LATAM expertise in consumer behavior at the base of the pyramid — a market that every major company is trying to figure out, and I've lived it. I'm also the kind of person who takes a 50% pay cut to build a skill I believe in. That's not desperation — that's conviction."

### Personal Questions

**Will you relocate? Are you willing to travel?**
→ "Absolutely. I've lived in Lima my whole life, and I'm excited to be in Chicago. I'm open to wherever the right opportunity is — US or LATAM. My background makes me effective in both."

**How does your background fit into...?**
→ Always connect your LATAM / engineering / PM / consulting background to the specific role. See positioning section.

**Who else are you interviewing with?**
→ Be honest about the general tracks (consulting + corporate strategy) without naming every firm. "I'm focused on consulting and corporate strategy roles — primarily at firms with strong consumer practice areas."

**Why are you better than another MBA candidate for this job?**
→ Three differentiators (see "Convince me" above). Don't be generic.

### Self-Assessment

**What is your greatest strength?**
→ Structural thinking + empathy. "I diagnose problems clearly and bring people along. Those two things together are rarer than either alone. At BCP, I didn't just design a better planning system — I redesigned it in a way that the people who would use it helped create. That's why it stuck."

**What is your greatest weakness?**
→ Be real. "Early in consulting I had to calibrate how deep to go in analysis given the client's decision timeline. My instinct is always to go deeper, but sometimes directional is what's needed. I've developed a new habit: start every analysis by asking 'what decision does this need to support?' before I start building."

**What was your greatest failure and how did you overcome it?**
→ GMAT story. Or the assumption-of-alignment failure at Favo.

**Where do you see yourself in 5 or 10 years?**
→ "In 5 years, I want to be in a strategy or consulting role where I own real outcomes — a client relationship, a business unit, or a strategic initiative. In 10 years, VP of Strategy or a GM role at a global consumer company, ideally with scope to develop their LATAM position. I want to be one of the people who decides where the company places its bets."

**Are you a team player?**
→ "Yes — and I'd say I'm particularly effective in teams where there's conflict, ambiguity, or misalignment. Those are the conditions where I've done my best work."

### Work-Related

**Why did you make your last move?**
→ The consulting pivot. Deliberate skill-building, 50% pay cut, 3-hour commute. "I wanted to learn structured problem-solving from the inside of a firm that had built it as a core competency. That's not something you can read about."

**What has been your most significant work accomplishment?**
→ BCP Warda launch — shipped after 2 years of prior failure, $15M→$25M asset growth.

**What did you like least about your last job?**
→ Be honest and professional. "At consulting, the limitation was that we delivered recommendations and then the client decided whether to act. I want to be the one accountable for both the decision and the outcome."

**What makes you think you have the analytical skills necessary?**
→ "Engineering degree where math was the daily medium. PM roles where I built and interpreted data products. Consulting where I produced market-sizing models and financial analyses. I'm not just analytically literate — I'm analytically fluent."

**Give me an example of a good ad campaign and tell me why it's good.**
→ Have 2-3 ready. See marketing.md for specific examples (Dove Real Beauty, Duolingo, Nubank). Evaluate on: target audience fit, message clarity, emotional resonance, distinctiveness, measurable business impact.

## Questions You Should Always Ask Them

At the end of every interview, have 2-3 thoughtful questions ready. Never ask about salary or benefits. Avoid questions easily answered by Google.

**Good questions:**
- "What's a project you're working on right now that you think will have the most impact on the organization in the next 12 months?"
- "What's the thing that surprised you most about working here, compared to what you expected when you joined?"
- "How do you see the LATAM market fitting into your growth strategy over the next 5 years? I ask because it's an area where I'd want to contribute."
- "What would make someone who joins as an intern stand out and earn a return offer?"
- "What does success look like in the first 90 days of the internship?"
