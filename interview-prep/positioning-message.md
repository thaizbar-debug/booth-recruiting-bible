## Exercise 1: Components of Your Message

**Who are you?**
Thaiz Barthelmess Miletich. Peruvian. Engineer + Businesswoman. MBA candidate at Chicago Booth (Class of 2028). Former product manager and strategic consultant with 5+ years building products and strategy for Latin America's largest bank, a high-growth startup, and a leading consulting firm.

**What's your career focus? Why?**
Management consulting and corporate strategy — specifically at firms and companies where rigorous problem-solving drives real business outcomes. Why: I've spent my career building things, and I've seen that the highest-leverage moments are when the right strategy is set, not just when execution is excellent. I want to operate at that level.

- *Prior experience that suggests this target:* At BCP, I designed the planning system that set strategy for a 30-person product team. At Apoyo, I delivered strategy to companies that needed it to survive and grow. This isn't a pivot — it's the through-line.
- *Environments I thrive in:* Ambiguity + cross-functional complexity + high stakes. Favo during the restructuring was chaos; I built structure in it. BCP Warda was "failed twice before"; I launched it. I'm most energized when the problem isn't well-defined yet.

**In what ways do you excel?**

*Individual contributor:*
I do the analysis myself, not just manage it. At Apoyo, I built financial models, conducted interviews, and wrote deliverables. At BCP, I ran A/B tests on product features personally, not through an analyst. I'm a practitioner, not just a coordinator.

*Team player:*
I'm the person who notices the misalignment before the meeting ends. At BCP, I redesigned how the tech and business teams communicated — not by adding more meetings, but by creating a shared language through the auction planning system. I remove friction from teams.

*Leader:*
I lead by making the problem clear and then giving people ownership. My best leadership moment was the Favo restructuring — no mandate, no playbook, a team in shock. I created structure where none existed and gave the remaining team something to move toward.

**What stories support these claims?**
1. BCP Warda: individual analysis + cross-functional team leadership + delivery
2. Favo restructuring: organizational leadership, ambiguity, team cohesion
3. Apoyo consulting: individual contribution, client management, structured deliverables
4. COVID WhatsApp: community leadership, influence without authority

**How have I prepared beyond coursework?**
- Strategic consulting at Apoyo (50% pay cut — deliberate preparation)
- Booth concentrations: Strategic Management + Marketing
- Pre-MBA case practice (target: 50+ cases before September 2026)
- Network of Booth 2nd-years and alumni at target firms

**Other things to convey:**
- LATAM expertise is a genuine differentiator — not a background story, an operational asset
- Engineering + Business double degree means I'm equally comfortable with data and with people
- My commitment is demonstrated by uncomfortable choices, not just by saying the right words

## Delivery Checklist (From Booth's Exercise 2)

Practice your positioning message in a mock networking conversation. Evaluate:

**Delivery:**
- [ ] Speaking clearly and with confidence (not rushing)
- [ ] Pace is natural, not memorized-sounding
- [ ] Eye contact / camera engagement (virtual)
- [ ] Energy level: engaged but not performative

**Content:**
- [ ] Easy to follow — no jargon, no unexplained acronyms (Favo, BCP, Apoyo need brief context)
- [ ] Relevant to the audience — what does this person care about?
- [ ] Authentic — does it sound like you?
- [ ] Ends with an invitation for dialogue — a question for them, not a monologue
