# Interview Prep — Strategic Planning & Business Development

Source: Booth Career Services FAQ (StratPlngBD-alumni)

---

## Interview Format

Typically **half fit / half case**. Strategic Planning applicants should prepare consulting-style cases focused on the company's industry. Business Development applicants should also prepare deal evaluation frameworks and financial modeling questions.

---

## Your Positioning

**For Strategic Planning:**
"I've designed strategy from inside large organizations — at BCP, I rebuilt how a 30-person product team sets priorities and allocates resources. That's strategic planning in practice. Booth MBA gives me the analytical toolkit to do this at a higher level of rigor and with broader organizational scope."

**For Business Development:**
"At Favo, my entire PM mandate was B2B — building the commercial platform for neighborhood stores to buy from suppliers. I understand deal structures, counterparty incentives, and what makes a partnership actually work at the operational level. My engineering background means I can evaluate technical integrations, not just financial terms."

## Additional Questions for Business Development

**Describe any prior financial modeling experience.**
→ Apoyo consulting: market sizing models, EBITDA projections, operational restructuring scenarios. BCP: unit economics modeling for Warda (customer acquisition cost, asset growth, retention curves). "I rate myself as proficient — I build models to answer strategic questions, not as an end in themselves."

**How would you propose and evaluate a deal?**
→ Framework: (1) Strategic rationale — does this advance our core position or open a new market? (2) Financial analysis — NPV, synergies, integration cost. (3) Operational fit — can we actually execute together? (4) Risk assessment — what are the failure modes? (5) Alternatives — what does organic growth look like instead?

**How would you negotiate a deal?**
→ "I start by mapping the counterparty's actual interests — not their stated position. At Favo, our supplier negotiations worked best when we understood that the small suppliers needed cash flow certainty more than maximum price. Once we structured payments around that insight, deals closed faster. I negotiate from understanding, not just leverage."

**What are the three most important things to make a deal occur?**
→ (1) Mutual economic logic — both sides must win. (2) Trust — deals fall apart when counterparties feel they're being gamed. (3) Operational clarity — who does what after signing? Most deals fail in execution, not negotiation.

## Strategic Thinking

→ Same deal evaluation framework above. Add: "I also ask 'what does success look like in 3 years?' — if the team can't agree on that answer, the deal isn't ready to evaluate yet."

## Leadership Skills

**Describe your leadership style.**
→ "I lead through clarity and psychological safety. I spend more time than most on making sure everyone sees the same problem before we discuss solutions. At BCP, that meant redesigning how we communicated priorities — not just having better meetings."

## Team Skills

**Tell me about your last team experience.**
→ Apoyo consulting project team. Or BCP cross-functional product team (tech, design, data, business).

**Tell me about a time your opinion was challenged.**
→ BCP: Tech leadership challenged my auction system proposal. Responded by designing a pilot rather than debating in the abstract.

**How would other members of your team describe you?**
→ "Direct but collaborative. I ask hard questions early so we don't discover problems late. I think they'd also say I'm the person who remembers the human side — during the Favo restructuring, I was the one making sure we treated the people we were letting go with real dignity."
