"I'm Thaiz Barthelmess, a Booth MBA student with a background spanning product management at Peru's largest bank and a high-growth startup, and strategic consulting. I took a deliberate 50% pay cut to move into consulting because I wanted to master structured problem-solving — and now I want to bring that rigor, plus my engineering + business lens and deep LATAM expertise, to [firm] where I can work on [consumer/CPG/social impact] challenges at scale."

## Fit with Consulting Role

**Walk me through your resume / Tell me about yourself. \*\***
→ Use your 60-second story. Thread: Engineer → PM at startup (Favo) → PM at bank (BCP Warda) → Consulting pivot (deliberate, 50% pay cut) → Booth MBA. Emphasize each move was intentional and upward in rigor.

**Why do you want to be a consultant? \*\***
→ "At BCP, I redesigned annual planning as an 'auction system' that forced business and tech to align — essentially consulting work inside a bank. At Favo, I built strategy under chaos. Consulting formalizes what I've been doing: rigorous problem-solving, with variety of industries, at the pace that keeps me sharp. I took a 50% pay cut to develop this skillset intentionally — this isn't a pivot, it's a destination I've been heading toward."

**Why did you choose Chicago Booth? \*\***
→ Emphasize: Booth's rigorous analytical culture (matches engineering background), Chicago consulting ecosystem (McKinsey, BCG, Bain all here), MBA structure that lets you specialize in Strategic Management + Marketing, and the global alumni network.

**How do your experiences qualify you for consulting?**
→ Three angles: (1) Engineering + Business degrees = analytical rigor AND commercial judgment. (2) Two PM roles = structured problem decomposition, cross-functional leadership, and client (internal stakeholder) management. (3) Consulting role (Apoyo) = 15 projects, $15MM EBITDA work, structured deliverables.

**Why did you decide to leave your previous career?**
→ Story: Consulting pivot. "I was earning well as a PM, but I realized the structured, hypotheses-driven problem-solving I admired in consultants wasn't something I could just read about — I had to practice it. So I took a 50% pay cut and a 3-hour daily commute to join Apoyo, Peru's most rigorous local consulting firm. I wanted to build the skill deliberately, even if it was uncomfortable."

**What scares you most about consulting?**
→ Be honest but frame forward: "The pace of context-switching — moving between industries quickly. But that's also what I've been training for. At Favo I went from e-commerce strategy to mass layoff management in weeks. At BCP I worked on financial inclusion tools for base-of-the-pyramid customers while simultaneously driving a $15M→$25M asset growth. I've learned to find my footing fast."

**Convince me that I should hire you. What makes you unique?**
→ Three differentiators: (1) Engineering + Business double degree — rare analytical + commercial combination at this level. (2) Base-of-the-pyramid product experience — real customer empathy at scale, not theoretical. (3) Deliberate consulting pivot — I didn't drift here; I chose discomfort to build this skill. Most candidates transition into consulting from banking or pure strategy. I built the skill from the ground up, by choice.

## Fit with the Firm

**Why do you want to join our firm? \*\***
→ Customize per firm. General frame: (1) Specific practice area (Consumer & Retail at McKinsey, Consumer practice at BCG/Bain). (2) Something you learned from a 2nd-year student or alum at that firm (mention the name). (3) A recent engagement or initiative that shows where you want to plug in.

**What makes our company different from competitors?**
→ Prepare this for each firm. Examples: McKinsey = breadth + social impact arm; BCG = BCG Henderson Institute + BrightHouse for brand purpose; Bain = private equity practice + results-orientation; Deloitte = size + public sector reach.

## Analytical Skills

**Tell me about a time when you had to convince someone using data. \*\***
→ Story: BCP Warda launch. "The product had failed twice in 2 years under previous teams. I ran A/B tests on gamification mechanics, showed a 50% increase in user engagement in the pilot, and modeled the asset growth trajectory from $15M to $25M with conservative assumptions. The data made the case — we launched and hit those numbers."

**Tell me about a time you solved a difficult problem in your most recent job.**
→ Story: Favo restructuring. "When Favo's Brazil offices shut and 300 people were laid off, I was one of the youngest PMs. I created a unified strategy committee across all remaining markets — designed the governance structure, facilitated cross-functional alignment, and held the team together while leadership was in chaos. The problem wasn't technical; it was organizational, and I solved it by creating structure where none existed."

## Leadership Skills

**What was your most significant leadership experience? \*\***
→ Story: BCP Warda launch. Led a cross-functional team that broke a 2-year impasse between business and tech. Redesigned the annual planning process as an "auction system" to create unified roadmaps. Launched in 10 months what previous teams couldn't ship in 2 years.

**Tell me about one of the toughest clients you've had. \*\***
→ Story: Apoyo consulting client (family business owner resistant to change). Frame: "The client had built the business over 30 years and was resistant to any recommendation that touched his organizational model. I spent the first two weeks understanding the business through his lens — the history, the values, the informal power structures. Once I reframed the restructuring around protecting his legacy rather than 'fixing' the company, he engaged completely. The relationship became collaborative."

**Tell me about a difficult interpersonal relationship. \*\***
→ Story: Favo mass layoff. "I had to tell team members their roles were being eliminated — including people I'd hired. What made it difficult wasn't the conversation itself but maintaining their dignity through it. I prepared individually for each conversation, gave full context (not euphemisms), and helped each person with their transition as much as I could. I also managed the anxiety of the survivors, who needed to see that leadership was stable and honest."

**Tell me about a time you failed.**
→ Story: GMAT resilience. "My first serious GMAT attempt didn't go as planned. I'd been preparing the same way I studied engineering — high intensity but without addressing the real bottleneck, which was test anxiety and time management under pressure. I failed to diagnose my own problem. When I stepped back, sought help from a coach, and rebuilt my approach around the actual weakness, my score improved significantly. The lesson was that diagnosing the right problem matters more than working hard on the wrong one."

**Tell me about a time you made the best decision despite less than perfect information.**
→ Story: Favo restructuring. "When Brazil shut down, we had 72 hours to decide which teams and products to preserve with severely reduced resources. No time for full analysis. I built a rapid triage framework — which products had highest retention, which markets had clearest unit economics — and made the call. Not all of it was right, but the framework held."

## Team Skills

**Describe a team experience that did not work well. \*\***
→ Be honest and specific, show learning. "Early in my time at Favo, I assumed alignment because no one pushed back in meetings. I later realized the Brazil team had a completely different mental model of the product roadmap. By the time I discovered the divergence, we'd built incompatible features. I learned to close the loop explicitly — not just ask 'does this make sense?' but 'walk me back through what you're going to build next.'"

**Describe a team experience that worked well. \*\***
→ Story: BCP Warda launch. "The key was the auction system we built for planning — it forced every team to compete openly for resources with data-backed proposals. That transparency killed the silo politics. When everyone could see the same priorities at the same time, alignment happened naturally. The team shipped a product in 10 months that had been stuck for 2 years."

**Tell me about a time you disagreed with your superior.**
→ Story: Consulting pivot. Or use a specific Apoyo example of pushing back on a client recommendation with data.

**Describe a time you had to convince a person or group to re-evaluate their decision.**
→ Story: BCP planning redesign. "Tech leadership had decided on a fixed quarterly roadmap system. I convinced them to try the 'auction' model by running a small pilot with one business unit — showing how it surfaced misalignment before it became a problem, not after. Once they saw the before/after, they advocated for it themselves."

## Additional Fit Questions

**Describe a situation where you had to work with ambiguity. \*\***
→ Story: Favo restructuring. 300 people laid off, leadership in chaos, no clear mandate. "I created clarity where there was none — defined the governance structure, aligned the remaining PMs on a unified strategy, and gave the team something to work toward. Ambiguity is just a problem without a clear owner. I become the owner."

**Describe an experience when you had to convince someone/a peer/a manager. \*\***
→ Story: BCP auction system. Or Favo unified strategy committee.

**What is something you are passionate about?**
→ "Building things that improve people's sense of self-worth. Warda was a savings app for low-income communities — we designed it to make saving feel like an achievement, not a chore. At Favo, we served mom-and-pop stores that were otherwise invisible to formal commerce. The work I'm most proud of is where strategy and human dignity intersect."

**What do you do for fun?**
→ Bodybuilding / fitness (1-year competition prep, discipline, community builder — COVID cycling classes, WhatsApp fitness challenges). Travel across LATAM. Learning through reading.

**On your last performance evaluation, what was something you needed to work on?**
→ Be specific and honest. Frame as growth: "Early in consulting, I had to work on calibrating the depth of analysis to the client's decision timeline. My instinct was to go deeper — but sometimes a client needs a directional answer in a week, not a perfect model in a month. I've learned to start with 'what decision does this analysis need to support?' rather than 'what would full rigor look like?'"

**How do you feel about working with colleagues 4-5 years younger or older?**
→ "I was the youngest PM at Favo at 22, managing people 10+ years older. I've also worked for senior partners in consulting at 25. Age is less relevant than clarity of roles and mutual respect. I've navigated both."
