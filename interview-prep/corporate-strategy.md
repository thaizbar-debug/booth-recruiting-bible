# Interview Prep — Corporate Strategy

Source: Booth Career Services FAQ (Corporate Strategy)

---

## Interview Format to Expect

Corporate Strategy interviews are typically **half fit / half case**. Final rounds usually involve a fly-in: 3–5 interviews over a Friday, often preceded by a Thursday evening social (treat it as an unofficial interview). Research the company's industry deeply — cases will likely center on their specific challenges and opportunities.

## Your Positioning for Corporate Strategy

**Headline:** "I've done internal corporate strategy work — at BCP, I rebuilt the annual planning architecture for a 30-person product team. At Apoyo, I advised companies on strategy from the outside. Corporate Strategy lets me combine both: insider credibility with consultant-level rigor."

**Key angles:**
- Analytical depth (engineering + finance background + consulting training)
- Cross-functional leadership experience (BCP tech/business alignment)
- LATAM market expertise (rare in US corporate strategy teams)
- Revenue-connected work (BCP asset growth $15M→$25M; Favo unit economics CAC $80→$5)

## Fit with Strategic Planning Role

**Walk me through your resume.**
→ Engineer by training → youngest PM at Favo (startup chaos) → PM at BCP (financial inclusion at scale) → deliberate move to consulting (Apoyo, 50% pay cut) → Booth MBA for Strategic Management + Marketing. "Each move was toward more rigorous, structured problem-solving with larger business impact."

**Why do you want corporate strategy?**
→ "Corporate strategy puts you in the room where the CEO's agenda is set. At BCP, my planning redesign had that visibility — it connected product decisions to bank-wide priorities. I want to operate at that intersection permanently: strategy that actually shapes how a company moves, not just advises from the outside."

**What are your thoughts on the evolution of this industry?**
→ Prepare per company. General themes: AI disruption of core products, ESG pressure, LATAM market expansion, private label growth (for CPGs), digital transformation of distribution, post-tariff supply chain restructuring (2025–2026 context).

**What do you consider to be the competitive landscape?**
→ Prepare per company. Use Yahoo Finance + SmartBrief + company's annual report before the interview.

**What do you perceive as the negatives about this company?**
→ Research-based, specific, forward-looking. Never generic ("execution risk"). Example: "Your LATAM expansion has been slower than peers — I think that's actually an opportunity, given the talent I bring, but I'm curious how the team thinks about it."

**What has been your most significant leadership experience?**
→ Story: BCP Warda launch. Led a cross-functional team of 30+ across tech and business. Redesigned how the organization makes product investment decisions. Shipped what two prior teams couldn't in 2 years.

**Describe a situation where you had to work with ambiguity.**
→ Story: Favo restructuring. No clear mandate after the Brazil shutdown. Created structure, defined the strategy committee, aligned the remaining team on unified priorities.

**Describe an experience when you had to convince someone/a peer/a manager.**
→ Story: BCP auction system. Convinced tech leadership to abandon their preferred quarterly roadmap in favor of a new system — piloted it first, let the data do the persuading.

**What are your short-term and long-term goals?**
→ Short-term: Summer internship in corporate strategy at [Company X], applying analytical rigor and cross-functional leadership to a strategic initiative. Long-term: VP of Strategy or Chief Strategy Officer at a global consumer company, with a focus on LATAM expansion strategy. "I want to be the person who decides where the company places its bets."

**Are you also considering consulting? Is strategic planning your backup plan?**
→ "Both tracks appeal to me for genuine reasons. Consulting gives me breadth; corporate strategy gives me depth and ownership. I'm pursuing both authentically. What I know is that I want to be solving the hardest strategic problems, and both paths get me there."

## Fit with the Firm

**Why does this company interest you?**
→ Specific: (1) A recent strategic move you find compelling. (2) A product/brand you know well. (3) A specific strategic challenge where your LATAM background is relevant. (4) Someone you met in a coffee chat who represents the culture.

**What do you think is the most important characteristic of someone on your team?**
→ "Intellectual honesty — the ability to be wrong in front of the room, update based on evidence, and not defend a position just because you built it. That's what makes strategic conversations productive." Give the BCP example of updating the plan mid-pilot based on data.

## Leadership Skills

**Describe your leadership style.**
→ "I lead through clarity and inclusion. I start by making sure everyone sees the same problem, then I open the floor — I've found that the best ideas often come from the least expected place. I had an engineer on the BCP team redesign our entire user flow during a planning session because I asked the 'dumb question.' Then I hold decisions firmly once they're made."

**What was the most challenging or riskiest decision you have made?**
→ Story: Consulting pivot (50% pay cut, 3-hour daily commute, moved back to parents' home). "I gave up salary, title, and comfort to build a skill I knew I needed. It was the right call."

**Tell me about a time you failed.**
→ GMAT story. Or specific project failure with clear learning extracted.

## Analytical Skills

**Tell me about a time you used data to convince someone.**
→ BCP Warda pilot data — A/B tests on gamification, engagement uplift, asset growth projection.

**Tell me about a time you've done financial analysis.**
→ Apoyo consulting — market sizing and EBITDA modeling ($15MM work). BCP — unit economics for Warda.

**How do the three major financial statements connect?**
→ Net income flows from P&L to retained earnings on balance sheet. Cash flow statement reconciles net income to actual cash using adjustments for non-cash items and working capital changes. Be prepared to walk through a working capital example.

## Case-Style Questions (Common for Corp Strategy)

**Joint venture / M&A / strategic partnership — what should we consider?**
→ Framework: Strategic fit (capabilities, markets), financial logic (synergies, integration costs), cultural compatibility, regulatory risk, exit scenarios.

**Business unit is experiencing declining profitability — what do you look at?**
→ Deconstruct: Revenue (volume × price) vs. Cost (fixed vs. variable). Then: competitive position, market growth, internal efficiency, customer mix.

**We are launching a new product — how do we size the market?**
→ Top-down (TAM → SAM → SOM) + bottoms-up (unit economics, per-channel). Size it, then question your assumptions.

**We are entering a new market with intense local competition.**
→ Porter's 5 forces on the target market + your company's right-to-win. "Should we enter at all?" is a valid first question.

**Divest a business unit — how do you decide?**
→ Strategic fit (core vs. non-core), financial performance, market attractiveness, buyer market, internal opportunity cost.
