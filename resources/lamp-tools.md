# LAMP Tools — Finding Companies and Contacts

Everything you need to build and execute your LAMP list: finding employers, finding contacts, finding email addresses.

---

## Finding Employers (Building the LAMP List)

### 1. LinkedIn Alumni Tool (Best Source)
**URL:** `linkedin.com/school/university-of-chicago-booth-school-of-business/people/`

Filter by: Company + Current students and alumni  
Use this to count Booth alumni at every firm on your LAMP list — that count is your **A column**.

**LinkedIn Groups Hack:** Add `&network=%5B%22F%22%5D` to any LinkedIn search URL to filter for **2nd-degree connections only**. This surfaces warm intro paths — people you can ask a mutual contact to introduce you to.

Example: Search "brand manager CPG" on LinkedIn, then append `&network=%5B%22F%22%5D` to the URL to see only people connected through someone you already know.

### 2. Crunchbase (For Growth Companies)
**URL:** `crunchbase.com`

Best for: tech-adjacent roles, growth companies, startups with MBA-level strategy roles.  
Search by industry, funding stage, location (Chicago for proximity advantage).  
Free tier gives limited searches — use for 5–10 lookups per day.

### 3. LinkedIn Jobs Search (P Column — Postings)
Search your target role keywords, filter by company. Count open roles at each firm to fill the **P column**.

Keywords by track:
- Consulting: `"MBA consulting associate"`, `"strategy consultant MBA"`, `"summer associate"` 
- CPG Brand: `"brand manager MBA"`, `"associate brand manager"`, `"brand management intern"`
- Marketing Strategy: `"marketing strategy MBA"`, `"growth strategy associate"`

### 4. Inc 5000 / Forbes Private / Chain Store Age
For finding less-obvious CPG and consumer companies that actively recruit MBAs:
- `inc.com/inc5000` — fastest-growing private companies
- `forbes.com/largest-private-companies` — mid-market firms with brand scale
- `chainstoreage.com` — retail and consumer brand news

## Finding Contacts at Target Companies

### 1. LinkedIn Alumni Tool (Start Here)
Same URL as above — filter by company to find Booth alumni specifically.  
Alumni have an **implicit obligation** to talk to fellow Booth students — response rates are much higher.

**Prioritize:**
1. Alumni who graduated 2–7 years ago (senior enough to be useful, junior enough to remember recruiting)
2. Alumni in the function you're targeting (not just the firm)
3. Alumni who are listed as "Open to" or who post on LinkedIn (more likely to respond)

### 2. LinkedIn Company Pages
- Go to the company page → "People" tab
- Filter by school: University of Chicago
- Also filter by function/title to find people in your target role

### 3. Booth Alumni Directory
Available through the Booth portal (requires your NetID after you start in September 2026).  
More complete than LinkedIn for Booth alumni — includes direct emails.

### 4. LinkedIn Groups
Search `[Company Name] alumni` or `[Company Name] MBA` to find group members.  
Group members are often more open to connection requests.

## Finding Email Addresses

### 1. hunter.io (Primary Tool)
**URL:** `hunter.io`

Enter a company domain → get the email format (e.g., `firstname.lastname@company.com`) + verify individual emails.  
Free tier: 25 searches/month. Enough for LAMP top 6 initial outreach.

**Workflow:**
1. Find person's name on LinkedIn
2. Go to hunter.io → search company domain → get format
3. Construct email from name + format
4. Verify with hunter.io's email verifier before sending

### 2. Email Format Guessing (When hunter.io Misses)
Try these formats in order — verify with hunter.io or by sending a test:
1. `firstname.lastname@company.com`
2. `firstname@company.com`
3. `flastname@company.com` (first initial + last name)
4. `firstnamel@company.com` (first name + last initial)

### 3. LinkedIn InMail
Fallback when no email found. Lower response rate than email, but still effective for alumni.  
Keep the message identical to your 6-Point Email.

### 4. Direct LinkedIn DM
For 2nd-degree connections: request to connect with a note, or message directly if they have "Open to" enabled.  
Use the 6-Point Email framework — same rules, just shorter.

## Company Research (Before TIARA Meetings)

### For Any Firm — 5-Minute Research Checklist

1. **Recent news:** Google `[company name] news site:wsj.com OR site:ft.com OR site:bloomberg.com` — last 60 days
2. **Annual report / earnings:** For public companies — investor relations page for last earnings call
3. **Glassdoor:** Read 5–10 reviews from people in your target function — gives you real insight for TIARA I questions
4. **LinkedIn:** Check your contact's posts and activity — comment fodder for connection lead
5. **Booth alumni insights:** Message a Booth alum who used to work there (even if they left)

### Consulting-Specific Research
- McKinsey Insights (`mckinsey.com/insights`) — published thought leadership in your target practice
- BCG Henderson Institute — BCG's research arm
- Bain Insights — sector-specific reports
- Deloitte Insights — functional/industry reports

### CPG-Specific Research  
- Nielsen consumer reports (summaries available free online)
- Kantar brand health trackers (published summaries)
- IFIC Food & Health Survey (annual)
- Progressive Grocer — trade publication
- Chain Store Age — retail/CPG industry news

## Booth-Specific Resources

### Career Services
- **Book in Week 1 of Year 1** — don't wait for recruiting season
- Career advisors assigned by track — consulting advisor vs. marketing advisor
- Mock interviews, resume reviews, firm introductions

### Clubs to Join (Join Week 1)
- **BCSC** (Booth Consulting Strategies Club) — case prep, firm events, alumni network
- **MBAMA** (MBA Marketing Association) — CPG recruiting, brand management prep, alumni
- **ALPFA** (Assoc. of Latino Professionals) — networking, diversity recruiting events
- **Net Impact** — social impact consulting opportunities

### Booth Events Calendar
- Firm info sessions (Oct 2026) — attend everything in your tracks
- Career fairs — meet recruiters, collect business cards
- Alumni panels — hosted by BCSC, MBAMA — high-value TIARA conversations in group format
- Treks — club-organized visits to firm offices (often include coffee chats with multiple contacts in one trip)
