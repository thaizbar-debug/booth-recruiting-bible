# Key Stories — STAR Format

These are Thaiz's most powerful recruiting stories, ready to adapt for behavioral interviews, coffee chats, and application essays.

---

## 1. Favo Restructuring — Leadership Under Ambiguity

**Situation:** At 22, youngest PM at Favo (100→500 employees), the CPO abruptly merged Product and Commercial teams without clear roles or vision. Silos deepened, VPs clashed, senior developers and designers felt lost.

**Task:** No one asked me to fix it — but someone had to, and I saw that I was positioned to bridge both sides.

**Action:** Scheduled 1:1s with both VPs and my decade-older PM counterpart. Listened deeply until every perspective felt heard. Where they saw irreconcilable conflict, I saw an opportunity for co-creation. Synthesized their input into the department's first unified strategy and a cross-functional committee structure. Presented it; the VPs approved and named me committee lead. When people publicly resisted in the first session, I stayed calm, acknowledged their frustration, reaffirmed that the decisions reflected everyone's input, and redirected us to the shared mission.

**Result:** Resolved a months-long organizational standstill. Created a unified team structure that held. Led cross-functional committee going forward.

**Use for:** Leadership, managing conflict, influence without authority, ambiguity, taking initiative

## 2. BCP Warda Launch — Breaking Silos Through Process Design

**Situation:** At BCP, joined a team of 30 where business and tech departments had worked in silos for two years. Most didn't know each other's names. Planning sessions lacked strategy and felt draining.

**Task:** Build a product roadmap that everyone felt ownership over — not just executed.

**Action:** Redesigned the planning process as an "auction." Business team co-created proposals with developers and aligned on technical feasibility before presenting. During workshops, everyone voted on initiatives. I translated winners into roadmaps, ensuring every voice shaped the direction.

**Result:** Broke the two-year silo. Shifted from confusion to shared vision. Everyone felt ownership. Launched Warda in the BCP app in 10 months after two years of prior failed attempts.

**Use for:** Process innovation, cross-functional collaboration, creating value, stakeholder management, creating impact

## 3. Favo Mass Layoff — Empathetic Leadership in Crisis

**Situation:** On a Friday, Favo's Brazil offices were shut down, 300 people laid off, 1,000+ entrepreneurs lost income. My entire team was let go, along with both my direct bosses, up to the cofounder and CTO. $50M burned, still far from break-even.

**Task:** Navigate my own uncertainty while helping manage the layoffs — and then keep the remaining team motivated.

**Action:** Helped with the layoff process when there weren't enough hands. Saw 30 additional resignations on top of layoffs. Realized that the hardest part for leadership isn't firing — it's managing the anxiety of those who remain. Scheduled 1:1s to understand personal motivations, helped people feel seen in their fears, communicated difficult decisions with empathy and clarity.

**Result:** Stabilized team sentiment during one of the company's worst weeks. Kept high-performing team members from leaving. Built a leadership style rooted in genuine human investment.

**Use for:** Resilience, dealing with failure, empathy, difficult situations, leadership in adversity

## 4. Consulting Pivot — Deliberate Discomfort

**Situation:** Had a stable, senior product manager role. Was making good money and had full independence (own apartment, established team).

**Task:** Recognized that my career needed strategic skills I wasn't building in operational PM roles.

**Action:** Accepted a consulting offer at 50% of my previous salary. Gave up my apartment, moved back to my parents' home. Committed to a 3-hour daily commute. Never mentioned my previous salary or seniority to new colleagues. Stayed humble, showed up early, opened and closed the office for the first six months.

**Result:** Built financial modeling, boardroom presentation skills, and strategic decision-making from some of Peru's most experienced consultants. Arrived at Booth with a 360-degree view of organizations — technical, commercial, and strategic — that very few MBA candidates have.

**Use for:** Growth mindset, long-term thinking, sacrifice for goals, intellectual humility, career transitions

## 5. Bodybuilding Year — Discipline and Mental Resilience

**Situation:** I had always set academic and career goals but never a physical one. I was curious how far I could push myself.

**Task:** Set a goal and follow through with zero compromise.

**Action:** Hired an Irish coach. Followed a year-long bikini-competition prep: 5am wake-ups, 20,000 daily steps, two-hour workouts, every gram of food weighed, not a single meal out.

**Result:** Beyond the physical transformation, I built unbreakable mental resilience that I still rely on daily. Proved to myself that I can commit to something with absolute depth. Now, whenever a challenging task makes me doubt myself, I return to that year of deliberate hardship.

**Use for:** Discipline, resilience, curiosity, comfort zone, personal goals, work ethic

## 6. COVID WhatsApp Challenge — Building Community

**Situation:** During Peru's strict COVID lockdown (one of the world's longest), I noticed people were growing bored, anxious, and isolated.

**Task:** Do something — not because anyone asked, but because I could.

**Action:** Posted an Instagram story inviting friends to a 30-day fitness challenge via WhatsApp. Participants submitted daily photo proof. I tracked progress in Excel. The group immediately maxed out the WhatsApp limit. Created a second edition on WeChat. Two full editions, 100+ participants each.

**Result:** Boosted community motivation and created belonging during one of Peru's hardest periods. Zero prize — people joined and stayed purely for community.

**Use for:** Initiative, community building, impact without authority, creativity, collaboration

## 7. GMAT — Failing Forward

**Situation:** Set a very high GMAT target score. Studied for a year while starting a new demanding consulting job, navigating a long-distance relationship, and relocating to a new home.

**Task:** Hit the score. Didn't.

**Action:** Instead of giving up or retaking the test blindly, I diagnosed the root cause: not content, but anxiety and time management. Sought out a psychologist and two specialized teachers. Practiced mindfulness. Restructured my study habits entirely.

**Result:** Didn't hit the perfect number. Achieved something better: a resilient and positive mindset, the ability to pivot under pressure, and the discipline to stay focused on the long-term objective. Got into Booth anyway.

**Use for:** Failure, asking for help, self-awareness, resilience, growth mindset, learning from mistakes
